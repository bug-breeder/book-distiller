# Practice Exercises: Chapter 5 — Copyright Page

## 🧪 Comprehension Check

**Q1:** The copyright notice states that the information in this book "is not intended as a substitute for consultation with healthcare professionals." What does this disclaimer reveal about the intended relationship between self-help reading and professional medical care?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

This disclaimer reflects a standard but meaningful boundary in health-related publishing: written guidance, however evidence-based, cannot replace individualized clinical assessment. Each person's health situation involves variables a book cannot account for — comorbidities, medication interactions, personal history — so the disclaimer signals that readers should use the book as a complement to, not a replacement for, professional evaluation and treatment.

</details>

**Q2:** The publisher grants purchasers specific permission to copy the ADHD Symptom Tracking Scale for personal use or use with clients, while prohibiting distribution on intranet or internet sites. What does this selective permission tell us about how the publisher views the scale's role?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

The selective permission treats the tracking scale as a clinical tool intended for direct, bounded use between an individual and their care context, not as freely shareable content. By allowing personal and client use but restricting digital distribution, the publisher is protecting commercial rights while still maximizing the scale's therapeutic reach — the goal is widespread individual application, not open redistribution that would undermine the book's value.

</details>

**Q3:** Two figures in the book are reprinted from other Guilford Press publications by Barkley and colleagues, with explicit permission credits. Why would an author reuse figures from their own prior works rather than creating new ones?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

When a figure has already been rigorously developed, peer-reviewed, and accepted in the scientific literature, reusing it ensures fidelity to the original validated representation of a concept. Creating a new version risks introducing inaccuracies or visual inconsistencies. The formal permission credit also signals intellectual honesty — even self-borrowing requires acknowledgment to maintain clear citation trails and copyright integrity.

</details>

**Q4:** The book exists in three distinct editions: paperback (ISBN 978-1-4625-4685-5), hardcover (ISBN 978-1-4625-4752-4), and EPUB (ISBN 978-1-4625-4755-5). What does the existence of multiple formats with separate ISBNs tell us about how publishers think about content accessibility?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

Separate ISBNs for each format reflect the publishing industry's recognition that format is not merely packaging — it constitutes a distinct product with its own distribution, pricing, and rights profile. For a health book on ADHD specifically, multiple formats matter practically: readers with ADHD may prefer digital formats for searchability and adjustable text, while clinicians may prefer physical copies for annotation. Accessibility across formats broadens reach to populations with different needs and resources.

</details>

**Q5:** The copyright is held by The Guilford Press (2022), not by the author Russell A. Barkley personally. What are the implications of institutional versus individual copyright ownership for how a work can be used, updated, or reprinted over time?
<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

When copyright is held by a publisher rather than an author, decisions about reprinting, translation, adaptation, and licensing rest with the institution rather than the creator. This matters for longevity: if Barkley wished to revise the book or release portions freely, he would need the publisher's consent. Institutional ownership also means the work persists commercially beyond the author's involvement, which can be beneficial for ongoing availability but may limit the author's ability to make time-sensitive updates or corrections independently.

</details>

## 🔄 Apply It

**Scenario 1: Using a Tracking Scale with a Client**
A therapist downloads the ADHD Symptom Tracking Scale from the Guilford Press website and prints 50 copies to hand out in a group ADHD coaching program. A colleague suggests uploading the PDF to the practice's shared intranet for easy access by all staff.

*What should you consider?*
- The copyright page explicitly permits copying for use with clients but prohibits storage or distribution on intranet sites.
- Printing for direct client use is covered; uploading to an intranet crosses into the prohibited distribution category even within a single organization.
- The safest compliant approach is to direct staff to the publisher's website to access the scale individually rather than hosting it internally.

<details>
<summary>Model Response</summary>

The therapist's individual printing for direct client use is explicitly permitted under the copyright terms. However, uploading to the intranet — even a private, internal one — falls under the prohibited category of "intranet sites." The right approach is to provide staff with the publisher's URL (www.guilford.com) so each person accesses the scale directly, or to print copies as needed for specific clients rather than pre-distributing digitally. This respects the publisher's rights while still enabling the scale's therapeutic use.

</details>

**Scenario 2: Relying Solely on the Book for ADHD Management**
A 34-year-old recently suspects they have ADHD after reading about the symptoms online. They purchase this book and decide to follow its recommendations strictly — adjusting their routines, diet, and environment — without seeking a formal diagnosis or consulting a doctor, reasoning that the book is by a leading expert and covers everything they need.

*What should you consider?*
- The book's own disclaimer states the information is not a substitute for professional consultation.
- Self-diagnosis based on symptom overlap can miss comorbid conditions (anxiety, sleep disorders, learning disabilities) that require different or additional treatment.
- Medication, which is one of the most evidence-based ADHD interventions, can only be prescribed and monitored by a qualified healthcare provider.

<details>
<summary>Model Response</summary>

The book's disclaimer exists precisely for this situation. While the behavioral and organizational strategies in the book have genuine value, using them without a professional evaluation means potentially missing a more accurate diagnosis or an effective medication component. A qualified clinician can also rule out conditions that mimic ADHD. The appropriate use of this book is as a complement to professional care — implementing its strategies while also pursuing a formal evaluation to confirm the diagnosis and explore the full range of treatment options.

</details>

**Scenario 3: Citing a Reprinted Figure in Academic Work**
A graduate student writing a thesis on ADHD wants to reproduce the figure from page 57 of this book (originally from Barkley's 2021 book "12 Principles for Raising a Child with ADHD"). They plan to cite only the current book since that is where they encountered it.

*What should you consider?*
- The copyright page explicitly attributes the figure's origin to the 2021 Guilford Press publication, meaning the primary copyright source is that earlier work.
- Academic citation standards require tracing to the original source; citing only the secondary source (this book) misrepresents the intellectual provenance.
- Reproducing the figure in a thesis may itself require separate permission from Guilford Press, distinct from the permission granted to the book's author for reprinting.

<details>
<summary>Model Response</summary>

The student should cite the original source — Barkley's 2021 "12 Principles for Raising a Child with ADHD" — as the primary reference for the figure, noting it was reprinted in the 2022 book. If the thesis will be published or publicly distributed, the student should also contact Guilford Press directly to request permission to reproduce the figure, as the permission granted to Barkley for his own book does not automatically extend to third-party academic use. Proper attribution protects academic integrity and respects the publisher's rights.

</details>

## 🗣️ Teach It Back (Feynman Technique)

**Prompt:** Explain the purpose of a copyright disclaimer in a health book in exactly 3 sentences to someone who has never encountered this idea before.

<details>
<summary>Model Explanation</summary>

A copyright disclaimer in a health book is a legal statement that tells readers the information inside is meant to inform and educate, not to diagnose or treat them personally. It exists because every person's health situation is unique, and a book — no matter how expert its author — cannot account for individual medical histories, other conditions, or medication interactions the way a doctor can. The disclaimer protects both the publisher from liability and the reader from over-relying on general guidance in place of care tailored specifically to them.

</details>
